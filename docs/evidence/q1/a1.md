# Q1 — Use case design

## Context

In the highly volatile environment of commercial aviation, Irregular Operations (IRROPS) represent one of the most significant operational bottlenecks and financial drains for an airline. This use case focuses on a prominent European low-cost carrier (LCC) operating a fleet of 180 Airbus A320 aircraft across a dense point-to-point network, managing over 600 daily flights. Because LCC business models rely on aggressive aircraft utilization with tight turnaround windows—frequently restricted to just 30 or 35 minutes—a single localized disruption can rapidly trigger a massive domino effect across the entire continent.

In the highly volatile environment of commercial aviation, Irregular Operations (IRROPS) represent one of the most significant operational bottlenecks and financial drains for an airline. This use case focuses on a prominent European low-cost carrier (LCC) operating a fleet of 180 Airbus A320 aircraft across a dense point-to-point network, managing over 600 daily flights. Because LCC business models rely on aggressive aircraft utilization with tight turnaround windows—frequently restricted to just 30 or 35 minutes—a single localized disruption can rapidly trigger a massive domino effect across the entire continent.

The core challenge is that the data required to resolve these multi-variable disruptions is fragmented into extreme technical silos across the company:

- Structured Real-Time Data: Live flight schedules, precise aircraft positioning, passenger booking payloads, and current crew duty rosters live inside a transactional, relational database.

- Unstructured Regulatory and Legal Data: The legal frameworks governing permissible recovery strategies live inside massive text repositories. These include dense EASA Flight Time Limitations (FTL) legislations (such as Regulation EU No 965/2012), intricate and highly specific labor union Collective Bargaining Agreements (CBAs) for pilots and cabin crew, airport ground handling Service Level Agreements (SLAs), and passenger compensation mandates under Regulation (EU) No 261/2004 (EU261).

Currently, OCC controllers must manually cross-reference real-time flight telemetry with these dense documentation binders to ensure that an emergency crew swap does not violate EASA maximum daily flight duty periods or break union rest-period agreements. This manual lookup takes hours, leading to prolonged delays, missed slot opportunities, and massive regulatory fines. Furthermore, the airline cannot feed this data into public cloud AI services like standard ChatGPT or Copilot; uploading proprietary crew rosters containing Personally Identifiable Information (PII) and highly sensitive corporate union agreements violates strict European GDPR mandates and corporate data security protocols.


## Personas

To understand how operational friction propagates through the network, we examine the workflows and specific pain points of two core personas within the airline's Operations Control Center (**OCC**).

### 1. Alejandro Vance — OCC Operations Duty Manager (Network Controller)

- **Role & Mission:** Alejandro is responsible for the overall operational integrity, on-time performance (**OTP**), and structural safety of the airline's fleet during his 12-hour shift. His primary goal is to minimize total network delays, prevent flight cancellations, and manage corporate cost exposure during major disruptions.
- **Operational Workflow:** Alejandro monitors macro network health using live radar feeds, Eurocontrol slot allocation screens, and weather infrastructure dashboards. When a storm system disrupts a primary hub like Paris Beauvais (**BVA**), he must immediately orchestrate a recovery plan. This involves deciding which aircraft tails to swap, choosing which flights must absorb delays, and evaluating whether an empty *ferry flight* is required to reposition an aircraft to protect the next morning's first-wave departures.
- **Pain Points & Friction:**
- **Decision-Making Paralysis:** During an **IRROPS** event, Alejandro is bombarded with competing constraints. He must balance Eurocontrol Calculated Take-Off Times (**CTOT**), airport curfew limitations, and structural maintenance windows.
- **Financial Blindspots:** He lacks immediate visibility into the financial downstream consequences of his network adjustments. For instance, he cannot easily calculate if delaying a flight by 45 minutes to wait for a late crew will push passengers past the critical 3-hour mark that triggers mandatory, non-negotiable **EU261** compensation payouts (€**250** to €**600** per passenger).

### 2. Elena Rostova — Senior Crew Rostering Controller

- **Role & Mission:** Elena manages the legal compliance, active assignments, and well-being of the 1,**200** pilots and cabin crew members across the airline's European operating bases. Her mission is to keep flights legally crewed while respecting labor laws and maximizing crew utilization.
- **Operational Workflow:** Once Alejandro proposes a network recovery layout (such as swapping two **A320** tails to mitigate a delay), Elena takes over to ensure the assigned crews are legally compliant. She reviews the current active duties, assesses standby (**SBY**) availability at the affected bases, calculates remaining allowable Flight Duty Periods (**FDP**), and updates the master crew management system.
- **Pain Points & Friction:**
- **Regulatory Complexity & Fragmentation:** Elena must manually cross-reference basic **EASA** **FTL** rules with highly specific, localized labor union Collective Bargaining Agreements (CBAs). For example, while **EASA** may legally allow an extended **FDP** under specific weather emergencies, the French pilot union agreement might strictly prohibit it, or the Spanish cabin crew **CBA** might dictate longer minimum rest periods if a split duty was worked.
- **Data Silos & Risk of Violations:** Legacy crew tracking software flag basic violations but fail to interpret complex textual clauses found in fresh union side-letters or temporary base agreements. Elena is forced to flip through **PDF** documents and internal wiki pages under extreme time pressure. A single miscalculation can result in an illegal crew assignment, forcing an aircraft to be grounded at the gate right before departure, creating severe union friction and heavy regulatory audits.

## Journey

When severe Air Traffic Control (**ATC**) slot restrictions delay flight **VY1234** from Paris Beauvais (**BVA**) to Barcelona (**BCN**) by **210** minutes, Alejandro and Elena use LobeChat to orchestrate a real-time recovery.

1. **Initiation & Intent Parsing:**
Alejandro types into LobeChat: *"Flight **VY1234** is delayed **210** mins at **BVA** with **172** passengers. Evaluate financial risk and find a legal standby crew swap."* A fast orchestration model (such as **GPT**-4o-mini) parses the prompt's intent.
2. **Structured Data Retrieval (Postgres via **MCP**):**
The model automatically executes an **MCP** database tool called `fetch_flight_manifest`. This tool queries the relational **Postgres** database to retrieve live operational data: the exact passenger load, connection vulnerabilities, and the specific crew IDs currently assigned to the delayed aircraft.
3. **Semantic Regulatory Search (Qdrant & MinIO via **MCP**):**
To assess legal constraints, the model invokes an **MCP** **RAG** tool called `search_compliance_rules`. This tool performs a semantic vector search in **Qdrant** against the raw regulatory documents stored in **MinIO**. It instantly retrieves targeted text paragraphs from the **EASA** **FTL** framework and the localized French Pilot Union Collective Bargaining Agreement (**CBA**) regarding maximum Flight Duty Periods (**FDP**).
4. **Advanced Reasoning & Roster Synthesis:**
A high-reasoning model (such as Claude 3.5 Sonnet) ingests both the structured data from Postgres and the unstructured text clauses from Qdrant. The model calculates that the current crew will exceed their maximum legal **FDP** by 18 minutes upon arrival in Barcelona.
To solve this, the model fires a subsequent **MCP** tool, `query_standby_crews`, scanning Postgres for available personnel at **BVA**. It identifies Standby Crew Bravo (Captain Martinez), cross-references his recent rest history against the union **CBA** to ensure absolute legality, and calculates that utilizing this crew reduces the departure delay to just 45 minutes.
5. **Streaming the Actionable Resolution:**
LobeChat streams the final solution onto the screen. The UI highlights that swapping to Crew Bravo maintains **100**% **EASA** compliance and prevents a €43,**000** **EU261** passenger compensation payout by keeping the final arrival delay well under the critical 3-hour legal threshold. Elena confirms the plan, and an **MCP** mutation tool pushes the updated roster directly back into Postgres.

## Why this stack vs ChatGPT/Copilot

This private, modular stack provides three decisive advantages over public cloud AI alternatives like generic ChatGPT or commercial Copilots within an airline's operational framework:

- **Absolute Data Sovereignty and **GDPR** Compliance:** Managing airline operations requires processing live crew rosters containing Personally Identifiable Information (**PII**) and highly sensitive corporate labor agreements (CBAs). Uploading this proprietary data to public cloud frontiers violates European **GDPR** mandates and corporate security protocols. This architecture runs entirely self-hosted within the airline’s secure **AWS** infrastructure, ensuring zero external data leakage.
- **Hybrid Data Synthesis via **MCP**:** Commercial enterprise AI assistants struggle to simultaneously orchestrate live, transactional relational data (Postgres **SQL** rosters) and dense, unstructured text regulations (**EASA** PDFs in MinIO and Qdrant). The Model Context Protocol (**MCP**) bridges this technical gap natively, enabling the **LLM** to smoothly query live operational tables and perform semantic vector searches in a unified, deterministic reasoning sequence.
- **Safe Operational Mutations:** Public AI tools operate as read-only informational tools or rely on generic, closed-ecosystem plugins. In contrast, this stack enables the airline to construct custom, sandboxed **MCP** tools. This architecture allows the model to not only read compliance data but also securely execute and commit the final, user-approved crew roster swaps directly back into the core Postgres database, transforming the conversational AI into an active operational agent.
